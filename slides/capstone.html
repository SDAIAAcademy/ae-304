How presentations are scored

The rubric

25%

20%
Access and freshness tests
20%
Agentic or structured-data component
20%
Reproducibility
15%
Show results from at least two measured runs. A claim without a number is a plan, not a result.

Walk through each criterion with one example of what full marks look like:

  • Retrieval quality and test set: a versioned test set with graded judgements, metrics by language and query type, and two measured runs compared (the Day 1 results table).
  • Corpus preparation: a parsing score by page type and a chunking comparison with a recommendation (Day 2, Sessions 1 and 2).
  • Access and freshness tests: leak, utility and staleness suites that pass, run by one command, including a changed document (Day 2 and Day 4 Session 3).
  • Agentic or structured-data component: traces and a comparison against single-shot search, or execution accuracy and a decision memo for statistics (Days 3 and 4).
  • Reproducibility: a clone, one install command and one run command reproduce the README's numbers; cost per question and p95 latency by stage are reported.
Format

8 minutes to present, 4 to answer

1 · The problem

Who, what, why now. One slide.

2 · What you built

Design, decisions, trade-offs. Then run one query live.

3 · What you measured

Baseline, improvement by language, one failure explained by component, what comes next.

Peer feedback. After each talk, everyone writes one strength, one question and one suggestion.

The defence is live. In the questions, add two or three queries of your own to the presenter's test set, ask them to run it, and ask them to explain one ranking: why this page is first. Check that the repository holds timestamped index-build and evaluation logs for the numbers in the README. The four minutes of questions are for this, not for a second talk.
